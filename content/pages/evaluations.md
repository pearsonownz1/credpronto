## What a credential evaluation is

A credential evaluation explains a degree earned outside the United States in U.S. terms. It names the closest U.S. equivalent, such as a bachelor's or master's degree, so a school, employer, licensing board or immigration officer can understand what you studied without knowing your country's education system.

## Which evaluation do you need?

**Diploma evaluation, $19.95 per degree.** This states the U.S. equivalent of your degree, for example "equivalent to a U.S. bachelor's degree in mechanical engineering." It's usually enough for employment, H-1B and other immigration petitions where the question is simply whether you hold the equivalent degree.

**Course-by-course evaluation, $39.95 per degree.** This lists every course on your transcript with its U.S. semester hours and letter grade, plus an overall GPA. Universities, community college transfers and many licensing boards (nursing, teaching, accounting and engineering, for example) usually ask for this one.

If you're not sure, check the requirements of whoever will receive the report. When they ask for semester hours, grades or a GPA, you need course-by-course.

## Common uses

- **Employment and H-1B petitions:** showing a foreign degree equals a U.S. bachelor's or higher
- **University admission and transfer credit:** course-by-course with GPA
- **Professional licensing:** state boards usually set their own requirements, so check the board's instructions first
- **Jobs and promotions:** employers that need proof of a degree level
